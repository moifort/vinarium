# AI and paid calls

## State the cost before building

Any feature that calls a paid API states its cost in its design document and in its commit
message before it is built: how many calls the first run makes, how many per day or month after,
multiplied out (rows × formats × languages × searches per call) and priced. A background pass is
sized to what a reader is shown, never to a whole dataset: looking up every winner since 1953 to
show a dozen books cost 526 calls and 25 euros in four hours.

## Gemini

- A grounded call bills about **four searches**, not one; the logged search count undercounts
  them. Searches are billed from the first one on a project that has spent its monthly free
  allowance (5,000 per GCP project).
- One API key per GCP project, on the project's own prepaid billing account, carried GitHub
  secret → Terraform → Secret Manager. Never a key shared between apps: one app's burst cuts
  every other.
- A local key, distinct from production's, set for every Claude session on the machine; before
  blaming production for a bill, compare fingerprints.
- Two limits, two errors: 402 when the prepaid balance is spent, 429 when the monthly cap is
  reached. The logged error says which.
- The invoice is the truth: read spend from the billing export in BigQuery, grouped by day and
  SKU; the grounded-search SKU is the one that costs. Structured logs (`jsonPayload.message`)
  with the step and the search count let a spike be attributed to a device and an hour.
- Past the free searches, grounded steps switch to Claude Haiku with web search capped at two
  searches per call; a monthly counter decides.
- Image generation for store panels: prototype at 1K or 2K, 4K only for a kept scene; the model
  draws dividing lines when asked for several panels in one image.

## Choosing a model

The method is the rule; the choices are a dated table, revisited at every major version of this
package and whenever a provider changes its prices or models.

- **A quality test before any change of model**, upgrade included, and before adopting a new
  one: the project keeps a bench script that runs the real task on about thirty real items
  (`scripts/bench-*.ts`), compares the candidates on quality and on cost per item, and the result
  goes into the commit message. A blind test by the person decides when quality is a matter of
  taste (recommendations, copy).
- **The cheapest model that gives the same quality wins.** At every change, the next cheaper
  model is in the bench; a more expensive one is adopted only when the bench shows the quality
  the cheaper one does not reach.
- A source that is free (a national library's catalogue) is benched against the paid model
  before the paid model is kept.

Choices as of 2026-10-21:

| Need | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Structured extraction from a photo or a page | Gemini Flash-Lite | Cheapest with vision, good enough for fields |
| Edition data with web grounding | Gemini grounded, then Claude Haiku + web search past the free tier | Cost of searches |
| Editorial recommendation from the person's own data | Claude Sonnet | Blind test won against Haiku, Opus and Gemini Pro; about 0.035 euro per generation |
| Choosing a month's picks, text only | Claude Sonnet | Same |

Google Books and Open Library are not edition sources (503 on most calls, wrong ISBNs, no French
editions); BnF's SRU serves French named books for free.

## A cap in production

Estimating before building does not protect from a loop or a burst. Every paid call, whether a
background pass or a user action triggers it, goes through a counter with a **daily cap per
project and per step** (Shiori's release watch: 30 a day); past the cap the step logs an `error`
and stops instead of continuing, and the daily health routine reports the counter. A user-facing
action is metered per user as well (a monthly quota on the free plan, an anti-abuse ceiling on the
paid one).

## Keys in the app

The app never holds an AI key: every call goes through the backend, which meters it (a free
monthly quota per user, an anti-abuse ceiling on the paid plan the app never shows).
